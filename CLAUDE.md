# CLAUDE.md

Guidance for working in this repository. Read `README.md` for the overview and `PLAN.md` for the current state; the manual (`make docs` → `docs/build/hearing-manual.pdf`) is the detailed record.

## What this project is

Two DIY open-ear **bone-conduction hearing aids** for the owner (UK, conductive loss from chronic wax blockages, glasses wearer). Per ear: clips on the glasses stem + a printed spring arm pressing a Dayton DAEX-9-4SM exciter on the mastoid, and a **gummy-bear earring** holding a small PCB (PDM mic + I2S class-D amp + LDO). One **XIAO nRF52840** in a **bumblebee necklace box** (carrier PCB + LiPo) listens to both mics and drives both amps. Everything is designed on screen from code; nothing has been built or powered. It is a hobby project, not a medical device.

## Golden rules

1. **Everything that is not source is generated by the Makefile and is gitignored.** Never edit or commit generated files (`cad/out`, `cad/print`, `pcb/out`, `pcb/carrier/out`, `pcb/fab`, `pcb/renders`, `docs/build`, `assets/body_upper.npz`, firmware build output). Two deliberate exceptions: `pcb/routes/*` (routing locks, below) and `docs/assets/` (hand-authored SVG diagrams, below).
2. **Never claim something works without checking its output.** Render it and *look* at the image, read the DRC report, run the validity checks. Earlier mistakes here were all things that looked plausible in a log (a stale preview, a floating part, a right ear inside the head).
3. **Be honest about status.** Parameters carry tags (DATASHEET, MEASURED, DERIVED, ESTIMATE, UNVERIFIED / ASSUMED, DESIGN). Do not turn an assumption into a fact; nothing has been tested on hardware.
4. **KEEP THE DOCUMENTATION UP TO DATE: this is part of every change, not a follow-up.** Whenever you change a decision, a number, a dimension, a part, a workflow, a Makefile target or a figure, update in the same piece of work: `PLAN.md` (state, decisions, TODO, history), the relevant chapter under `docs/manual/`, `README.md` (setup, targets) and this file if tools or conventions changed. Before finishing, ask "what would a reader of PLAN.md, README.md or the manual now get wrong?" and fix it. Cite third-party material through `sources.toml` (`[@key]` in the manual). Do not leave the docs for later.
5. **Ask before** spending the owner's money, ordering anything, running downloaded binaries beyond the router jar already approved, or committing/pushing (no commits have been made; do not commit unless asked).

## Workflows

- **Change a dimension** → edit the parameter → `make print-files previews` (then `make print-audit` measures the real wall thickness: the percentages and the 1st-percentile wall are meaningful, the minimum is not, because every sharp edge is a wedge) (and `make docs` for blueprints). `cad/print_files.py` validates every STL (valid single solid) and fails loudly in the manifest (`CHECK`).
- **Change a PCB** → edit `build_dangle.py` / `carrier/build_carrier.py` (+ `finish*.py` for silkscreen) → `make pcb`. If the placement fingerprint no longer matches the lock in `pcb/routes/`, the pipeline re-routes and writes a *new* lock: **review the DRC report and renders, then commit the new lock files**. `make reroute` forces it.
- **Add a figure** → write `docs/figures/<group>.py` using `docs/figures/common.py` (`save(fig, group, name)`), add the group to `FIG_GROUPS` and a stamp rule in the Makefile, reference it from a chapter as `figures/<group>/<name>.png`.
- **Add or edit a hand-drawn diagram** → `docs/assets/*.svg` (plain text, checked in, edited by hand; conventions and palette in `docs/assets/README.md`). Chapters use `![caption](assets/name.svg)`; `make docs` converts to vector PDF with `rsvg-convert`. Render it with `rsvg-convert -w 1280` and look at it. When a design change touches something a diagram shows (a pad, a part position, a length), edit the SVG in the same piece of work. Data-derived pictures stay generated under `docs/figures/`.
- **Refer to manual chapters by name, not number**: `[Parts and cost](#parts-and-cost)` (pandoc makes the id from the heading), because chapters get reordered. The manual has three parts (project, build and use, reference); new explanatory material goes in the first two, look-up tables in the third. Explanatory sections are written as a chain of constraints the reader could have discovered, not as history.
- **Add a source/citation** → entry in `sources.toml` (add `fetch` + `dest` for files that should be downloaded), cite `[@key]`.
- **Record an order or change an estimate** → `docs/data/orders.toml`: one `[[order]]` per movement of money, line items with a category, discounts as negative lines; a `paid` order must add up to its invoice total (the build checks). Tables and the by-order / by-category charts regenerate. **Never copy personal details from an invoice** (name, address, email, phone) into the repo: only dates, references and amounts.
- Before finishing any change run `make check` and look at the images you changed. After a figure change run `make docs` and look at the figure *in the PDF* at page size (`pdftoppm -r 60`), because that is the size a reader sees.

## Conventions and gotchas

- Units are **millimetres**. CAD world coordinates for the head model: X out of the **left** ear canal (origin there), Y toward the nose, Z up. **The body midline is at x = −60**, not 0: mirror about it (`2·XM − x`), centre things on it.
- Bear frame: X right as seen from the front, Y up, Z from the back plate (0) toward the front. Board→bear: `bear_x = −(board_x − 6.5)`, `bear_y = board_y − 13.5`. Bee frame: Z from the back (0) to the cover side; the preview maps it to world as (X, Z, Y).
- Dangle and carrier PCBs share one 8-pad order (1 LRCLK, 2 DIN, 3 VBAT, 4 PDM_DATA, 5 BCLK, 6 GND, 7 PDM_CLK, 8 GND) so the cable is a 1:1 harness.
- **KiCad's DSN export is not reproducible** (random item IDs reorder it) and the Freerouting result depends on that order, hence the committed routing locks. Freerouting itself is deterministic for a given DSN, but *every re-route is a dice roll*: the same placement can route cleanly or fail (an unconnected ground pad) on the next try. When you change a placement, re-run `REROUTE=1 make dangle-L` (touch `pcb/build_dangle.py` first, or `make` thinks nothing changed) until it routes and the DRC is clean, then commit the lock. Environment overrides `C3X/C3Y/C5X/C5Y/Q1X` exist for trying positions. **Small fixes to a routed board do not need a re-route:** put them in `pcb/routes/<board>.edits.json` (`pcb/route_edits.py`: move_via, delete_via, delete_track, add_track; stage `ses` before the ground plane, `ground` after it), tied to the placement fingerprint; the pipeline stops rather than re-route when a lock plus its edits fails DRC.
- pcbnew SWIG objects become invalid after the board is reloaded or modified through a second handle; re-load the board in a new script rather than holding objects across steps. Text fonts: `PCB_TEXT.SetUnresolvedFontName("Helvetica")`.
- `build123d`: a boolean on a `Part` that has become a `Compound` fails with "Dimensions of objects … inconsistent": build the shape (hook loops, ridges) into the solid *before* hollowing, and validate with `.is_valid` and `len(.solids())`.
- Matplotlib 3D does not depth-sort across separate collections: put everything that should occlude into one `Poly3DCollection` (see `common.draw_tris`).
- The software renderers are rough previews, not photographs; `kicad-cli pcb render` is the PCB reference.
- No auto-commits; generated outputs in `git status` mean a `.gitignore` entry is missing.

## PCB manufacturing checks (learned the hard way)

- The checker that matters is **JLCDFM** (the DFM Check button in JLCPCB's gerber viewer; free account). Run it on the zips in `pcb/fab/` after any PCB change and open every red and orange item: the table in the manual's Electronics datasheet is the record. It lives behind a login, so you need the user's logged-in browser.
- Steps that run after Freerouting (ground vias in `ground_plane.py`, the mic ring bridge in `import_ses.py`, silkscreen in `finish*.py`) add copper and text the router never saw. Tell the router about them (keep-out zones in `build_dangle.py`, `silk_zones.py`) or you get conflicts that move with every re-route.
- Thresholds that bit us (JLCDFM): via pad to via pad and via to line ≥ 0.15 mm (0.1 is danger), annular ring ≥ 0.15 mm (we use 0.2), silkscreen to pad/hole ≥ 0.18 mm, silkscreen stroke ≥ 0.153 mm, through-hole to SMD pad ≥ 2.03 mm danger / 3.05 mm good (wave-soldering rule; we keep ≥ 2.4 mm only for the exciter pads).
- CPL rotations are KiCad's raw orientations plus a per-footprint correction where JLCPCB's package model differs: the standard `SOT-23` needs +180° (found in the assembly preview by comparing the pin-1 dot with ours). Check every new polarised footprint in the preview.
- KiCad's python returns bare SWIG objects for some library footprints; edit the `.kicad_pcb` text instead (`pcb/silk_strip.py`).

## Figure legibility rules (learned the hard way)

- Draw figures at about page width (9 in for diagrams and charts) with 10 pt or larger text; never a 16 in canvas that the PDF shrinks to 40 %. Stack panels vertically (`plt.subplots(n, 1, ...)`) instead of side by side.
- Look for colliding text in every figure you touch: labels wider than their box, subtitles on top of pins, callouts on top of parts, dimension text over annotations, labels on the board edge, warnings over models. Fix the layout rather than shrinking the font further. Light text on dark parts (PCB green, dark chips), dark text on paper.
- Use the helpers: `common.box` (auto-fits its label), `schem.Sch.ic` (wraps titles, prints `WARNING schematic:` on collisions), `assembly.warn` (wraps text inside the page), `draw_tris(..., zoom=...)` for 3D cells.
- Tone of the prose: a factual description, not a pitch. Calling the earrings "gummy bears" and the pendant "the bee" is fine; no marketing headings.

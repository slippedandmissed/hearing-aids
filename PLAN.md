# PLAN.md: running state of the project

Last updated 2026-11-21 (end of the documentation and figure-readability pass). The manual (`make docs`) is the detailed record; this file is the short, current status for whoever (human or assistant) picks the work up next. See `CLAUDE.md` for how to work in the repo.

## Goal

Two independent open-ear bone-conduction hearing aids for temporary help on bad-wax days, plus fun and learning. Worn as: glasses-stem clips with a spring arm pressing an exciter on the mastoid, a **gummy-bear earring** (pink left, blue right) holding the microphone and amplifier PCB, and a **bumblebee necklace box** with one XIAO nRF52840 and a LiPo. Not a medical device.

## Architecture (decisions that hold)

- Bone conduction at the mastoid; exciter pressed by a printed PA12 spring arm (2.4 / 2.8 / 3.2 mm variants) fused to ONE **spring-jaw clip** on the glasses stem at 127 mm (V-shaped jaws in three fingers each, grips N nominal and T tight; designed for flat arms about 3.2–4.2 mm thick (grip T 2.9–3.9), ESTIMATE, untested). Why compliant jaws (see `docs/assets/clip-reasoning.svg`): the pad lever is 45 mm over a 14 mm clip, so any slack is tripled at the pad, and the stem tapers, twists about 5° across the clip and bends 41° just ahead of it, so the clip grips on thickness with spring force instead of an exact fit. The stem is MEASURED with calipers (2026-11-21): 3.54 mm thick, 6.40 mm at the hinge, 4.7 mm at the end of the straight section, 9.36 mm at the paddle. Stations: straight section 105.33 mm (measured); tallest point 134.72 mm from the hinge in a straight line, modelled at 135 mm along the stem. Design rule found while measuring: the arm must hang from the clip's shelf, not from a jaw (a fused jaw cannot spring).
- Digital signal path: SPH0641LM4H-1 PDM mic (in the dangle) → XIAO nRF52840 (PDM→16.129 kHz PCM, DSP, I2S) → MAX98357A class-D amp (in the dangle) → 2 × 22 Ω hardware power cap → DAEX-9-4SM. **One** XIAO serves both ears (stereo PDM/I2S); 8 wires per ear in two 4-wire silicone ribbons (VBAT, 2 GND, PDM clk/data, I2S ×3), same pad order on both PCBs.
- Dangle PCB 13 × 28 mm, all SMD on top, bottom = ground plane + big L/R + signature; L/R variants differ in R_SD (2 k / 220 k) and the mic SELECT strap.
- Carrier PCB 56 × 40 mm (r 9 mm corners): JST-PH → AO3401A reverse-polarity P-FET → SK12D07VG4 switch → 500 mA PTC → VBAT; LED; ten 33 Ω series resistors; two 8-pad ear blocks; XIAO soldered straight into the board (battery wired to its back pads); XIAO's own charger via its USB-C (works only with the switch ON).
- Bee box: thorax 62 × 46 × 15.8 mm (board), abdomen 44 wide × 10.1 mm (battery pocket), head ball with the chain hole, cosmetic flat wings, raised stripe bumps; overall 105 × 116 × 16.9 mm. Bears 32 × 46 × 7.6 mm with screw posts, mic ring, PCB stops, strain pegs; all cables leave through one top slot.
- Paint rather than multi-colour printing; clips, arms and bee base are dyed black MJF PA12 and stay unpainted.
- Tooling: build123d, KiCad 10 (+ Freerouting 1.9.0), Rust/embassy-nrf, pandoc, Makefile (no `just`).

## Open risks (what would settle each)

1. Amplifier rejects 16.129 kHz (first power-up: start-up jingle audible, last note at 1008 Hz on a tuner app; no scope owned). Fallback: another clock plan or firmware resampling.
2. Mic clock only 0.8 % above its 1.024 MHz minimum.
3. 8 digital wires × 60 cm as a shared bus (fallback: two XIAOs, no PCB change).
4. Feedback between exciter and mic (simulated only, on assumed paths: see the Firmware chapter); bone-conduction coupling quality; weight on the lobe.
5. Assumed part heights (JST 7.8, XIAO header plastic 2.5, battery 5.5 mm), stem dimensions.
6. JLCPCB specifics not yet checked: CPL rotations, 0.5 mm NPTH, mic stock (about 85), extended-part fees, Helvetica signature in the silkscreen preview.

Done 2026-11-23: `hearing-desktop feedback` closes a synthetic exciter-to-microphone path (two assumed paths, "damped" and "ringy", peak coupling set by `--coupling-db`, optional drift half way) around the real chain and finds the highest gain before it howls, with and without the canceller, plus a fidelity measure (output against the no-feedback ideal). Canceller: NLMS, up to 64 taps after a delay. **Finding: a plain always-adapting NLMS raises the stable gain by 13-19 dB but cancels the wanted sound (voiced fidelity 12 dB -> 3 dB), because the output is the amplified input.** Design chosen instead: **boot calibration** (3 s of quiet noise the device makes itself, fast NLMS), then a filter held close to the calibrated one (mu 0.002, leak 0.001 per sample toward it). Simulated result: +4 to +16 dB of stable gain depending on path and input, +4 to +7 dB when the path drifts, voiced fidelity within 1.5 dB of the chain without a canceller (better where feedback coloured the sound). The trade-off between tracking and speech bias is a monotone curve in the simulator (faster = more tracking, more damage); the fix would be a decorrelated adaptation signal (probe noise, or adapting only in quiet), not done. All of this is against **assumed** paths. The delay setting must be below the real onset of the path (too high loses most of the benefit; too low is harmless), so `CONDUCTIVE_GUESS` uses 0 until it is measured.

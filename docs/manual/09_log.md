# Project log

A dated record of decisions and findings, newest last. The detailed running notes are in `PLAN.md`; this is the readable version.

## 2026-11-20: concept to a complete design on screen

**Starting point.** Chronic wax blockages in both ears; wants an open-ear bone-conduction aid, low latency, a decorative form factor, 3D-printed by a service, PCBs by JLCPCB, budget about £200, UK based, through-hole soldering only, no calipers, no printer.

**Concept changes** (each replaced the one before):

1. *Tragus clip with an analogue chain* (microphone, AGC, tone, amplifier). Rejected: a clip on the tragus will not stay on.
2. *Ear-cuff on the helix with a compliant arm to the mastoid.* Superseded when the owner asked for everything to be tunable digitally.
3. **Digital-first, glasses clip, necklace box.** The exciter sits on the mastoid, held by a printed spring arm that clips to the **glasses stem**. The microphone is in an **earring dangle**. A processor in a necklace box is connected by thin cables that follow the chain.
4. **One XIAO nRF52840 for both ears** (its PDM and I2S blocks are stereo) instead of one per ear.
5. The dangle holds the microphone, a class-D amplifier and a 3.3 V LDO on one small PCB; the exciter cable is short and the neck cable has 8 conductors.

**Parts verified on listings:** MAX98357A amplifier (C910544), SPH0641LM4H-1 microphone (C497724), XC6206 LDO (C5446), Dayton DAEX-9-4SM exciter (13 × 26.5 × 5.75 mm, 2.3 g), SK12D07VG4 switch (500 mA; the MSK12C02 was rejected at 50 mA).

**Firmware feasibility.** embassy-nrf has async PDM and I2S drivers; a stereo passthrough compiles (13.7 KB). Not run on hardware.

**PCBs.** Dangle L/R and carrier laid out in code, routed with Freerouting, DRC clean (no unconnected nets).

**Housing findings.** A socketed XIAO would have made the necklace box 24 – 29 mm thick; **soldering the XIAO pins straight to the carrier** gave 16 mm. Moving the battery to a thin wing below the board kept the aspect ratio under 1.6:1.

## 2026-11-21: looks, print files and repository

- **Cosmetics.** Paint wells instead of multi-colour printing. Left ear pink, right ear blue ("bubblegum candy"). The dangles became **gummy bears**; the necklace box a **cartoon bumblebee** with a ball head carrying the chain, raised stripe bumps and flat cosmetic wings.
- **Internal supports for the bear**: screw posts, mic sealing ring, PCB stops, strain pegs, all cables out of the top.
- **Assembly preview.** Both ears, silver chain, grey clips, a glasses front built from photos. Two real bugs were found by looking: the body model's midline is not at x = 0 (the right ear was inside the head), and the first cat-eye outline was too pointy and partly inside the face.
- **PCB finish.** Carrier corners at 9 mm radius; ear pad blocks moved to clear the corners and the switch; the signature "morgan" in Helvetica added to all three boards.
- **Print files.** 58 STLs: clips in a fit matrix per ear, spring arm in three thicknesses, bears (L/R), bee. A validity check found a real defect (a floating friction lip on the bee cover), now fixed.
- **Repository restructured.** Stale code deleted; every output is generated by a Makefile and is gitignored; this manual is generated by `make docs`.
- **Router reproducibility.** Freerouting itself is deterministic (six runs on one design file gave identical routing), but KiCad's export of that file is not (random item IDs reorder it) and the router's result depends on the order: four exports of the same placement routed with 49 %, 51 %, 50 % and 0 % optimisation gain. The accepted routing is therefore committed as a small lock file per board (`pcb/routes/*.ses`) guarded by a fingerprint of the placement; `make reroute` replaces it. The "Start (20 s)" dialog is skipped with the router's `-dct 0` option.

## 2026-11-22: manufacturing checks and the first order

- **Calipers and JLC3DP limits** (2026-11-21, applied): stem measured; clip matrix recentred; bear screw posts 3.4 mm, PCB stops 1.0 mm, engraving 0.8 mm deep, exciter pocket clearance 0.45 mm.
- **JLCDFM, two rounds.** JLCPCB's free checker was run on all three boards; every red and orange item was opened. Changes: 0.7 mm vias (0.2 mm annular ring), ground vias 0.2 mm from pads, flat exciter resistors, silkscreen outlines of SMD parts removed, pad digits and text moved or thinned to 0.17 mm, router keep-outs for the mic ring bridge and the bottom text. What is left, and why it is acceptable, is in chapter 3.
- **Placement files wrong, then right.** JLCPCB's assembly preview showed the parts 28 mm above the board: the CPL counted Y from the bottom edge, the gerbers from the top. Fixed (`Y = -y`). On the carrier the transistor Q1 (reverse-polarity P-FET, the standard KiCad `SOT-23`) had JLCPCB's pin-1 marker half a turn from ours, which would have fitted it backwards; the CPL now adds 180° for that footprint. The LED polarity was checked in the same viewer.
- **Hand edits survive the router.** Every router run is a dice roll, so small fixes (move a via, delete or add a track) are kept in `pcb/routes/<board>.edits.json` and applied after the committed routing lock is imported.
- **Print audit and screws.** A mesh-level audit of every STL (`make print-audit`) found thin floors under the bear screw heads (0.5 mm), the cable notch (0.5 mm) and the clip engraving (0.8 mm). Fixed: shallower counterbore, shallower notch, engraving 0.6 mm deep. The audit also showed the bee box's M2 × 8 screws would have poked 2.8 mm out of the back; the screws are now M1.6 × 4 (bears) and M2 × 4 (box), and the pilot hole continues into the base wall. Cable: 30 AWG silicone ribbon (0.8 mm wires).
- **JLC3DP quote.** A quote of 10 representative parts gave the price rule (a $0.95 minimum per file, then about $0.34 per cm³; black dye about $0.10 extra) and JLC3DP's own thin-wall check, which flagged only the bee cover. Chasing it found a 0.3 mm strip of wall under the switch window and slivers of lip behind the cable slots; both were cut away. Colours: clips, arms and bee base dyed black; bears and bee cover natural grey.
- **First order placed** (three assembled PCB orders, one parcel, ordered in the JLCONE desktop app for a discount). £94.84 paid; the cost chapter became a ledger of orders with categorised line items.

## Decisions at a glance

| decision | alternatives considered | why |
|---|---|---|
| Bone conduction on the mastoid | tragus (cartilage) clip, helix cuff | the clip would not stay on; the mastoid is a large firm surface |
| Digital signal processing | analogue AGC chain | tuning without a soldering iron; app later |
| One XIAO for both ears | one per ear | half the cost and one battery; risk is the long shared bus |
| Microphone in the earring, processor in the chest | everything in the ear | weight on the ear and thin cables |
| Soldered XIAO pins, battery in a wing | sockets, thick box | 24 – 29 mm versus 16 mm thick |
| Hardware power cap of 2 × 22 Ω | firmware limit only | protects against firmware bugs |
| Clips in a size range | measure first | no calipers; cheaper than a wrong guess |
| Paint, not colour printing | full-colour nylon, dye | MJF is grey or black only; colour nylon is muted and dearer |
| Makefile | justfile | file-based dependencies; no extra tool to install |
| Commit the router result as a lock | regenerate every time | the router input is not reproducible, so a rebuild could route worse |

- **2026-11-22, lean print set chosen.** Clip stem thickness B and C only (34 single files, about £58–68 with shipping and VAT; the full set would be £82–93), after two re-measurements of the stem thickness fell inside the go/no-go window. Optional bundle files exist, but JLC3DP flags them as assemblies. Fixed the section 3.5 battery chart (its two-column legend squeezed the axes to nothing at the manual's 65 % shrink) and the bee base colour in the section 4.6 overview.

- **2026-11-22, clip redesign.** Investigating whether the clip at 127 mm could stabilise the arm on its own showed that clip A (at 100 mm) was a separate part and could not help, that an exact-fit rectangular clip loses preload to its own slack (pad lever 45 mm over a 14 mm clip), and that the stem bends 41° between the clips with its faces twisting about 5°. Replaced both clips and the twelve-size fit matrix by one **spring-jaw clip** per ear (V-shaped jaws in three fingers, grips N and T) and moved the arm onto a shelf on the top plate: in the first version the arm ran through one jaw over almost its whole height and would have made it rigid. Print set: 14 files, about £26 in goods. Also fixed on the way: the stem profile figure drew the stem unrolled (it looked straight; it is now a side view), and the JLC3DP quote rejected multi-clip files as assemblies. All grip numbers are ESTIMATES from a cantilever model; nothing has been fitted to the glasses.

- **2026-11-22, JLC3DP heatmap and rename.** The user pasted the viewer link for the uploaded clip: JLC3DP's thin-wall heatmap flagged every jaw finger yellow (under 1.2 mm) with some red slivers, while the arm and plate were grey. The mesh probe agreed (44 % of the clip surface under 1.2 mm with 1.2 mm jaws, 2.9 % with 1.4 mm), so the jaws are now **1.4 mm**: grip about 8 N (N) and 11 N (T) per jaw at the measured 3.54 mm stem and 1.0 / 1.4 % strain, usable stems about 3.2 – 4.2 mm (N) and 2.9 – 3.9 mm (T). With one clip left, "clip B" became "the clip": files are `clip_arm2.8_L_N.stl` etc., and the engraved IDs are L/R plus the grip letter (`LN`, `RT`). Also found: JLC3DP's quote list holds at most 10 lines, so the 14 files go in two quotes. The heatmap of the 1.4 mm version (checked for every part of the first quote through a hooked `window.open` in the browser) showed no red on the jaws but yellow finger tips and small red patches at the engraved arm digits (strokes only about 0.5 mm wide), so the lead-in was cut from 0.6 to 0.4 mm (tips 1.0 mm), the plate digits went from 5 to 6.5 mm bold, and the STL export now drops stray single-triangle fragments (the clips are single watertight meshes). To be rechecked after re-uploading.

- **2026-11-22, prints in the cart.** Refinements after the heatmap check: jaw lead-in 0.4 mm (tips 1.0 mm), plate digits 6.5 mm bold, stray single-triangle fragments removed from the STL export (every file is now one watertight mesh). The heatmap of all eight clips (read through a temporary `window.open` hook in the browser) shows no red on the jaws, yellow on the finger faces and yellow/red outlines on the engraved plate digits; the bear parts are mostly yellow (back plates) with a red spot at the mic hole. None of it raises JLC3DP's 'Printing risk' flag except the bee cover. All 14 files are in the cart; the order is not placed.
